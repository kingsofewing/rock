⚠️ Trip safety — read once before you go

🐕 Blue-green algae (the #1 dog danger): Lake Winnipeg (and other warm lakes) bloom with toxic cyanobacteria in summer. A tiny amount can kill a dog within hours — there is no antidote. If water looks like pea soup, spilled paint, or has a blue-green scum, keep the dog out and don't let him drink lake water near shore.
🌊 Lake Superior: ~40°F year-round → cold-water shock in minutes, plus rip currents that drown people every year. Treat it like an ocean: wade, don't swim out; no one in the water during wind/wave warnings.
🪳 Ticks & Lyme: WI/MN northwoods are among the highest Lyme/anaplasmosis areas in the country. Repellent, long pants in brush, and a full tick check (people + dog) every night.
🐻 Bears & 🫎 moose: store all food/scented items in the car (never the tent); keep distance. Moose injure more people than bears — never get between a cow and her calf.
🦌 Dawn/dusk driving: moose and deer on the road are a real collision risk on the northern legs — slow down at low light, especially Ely → Sioux Narrows and the Canada legs.
🌿 Plants: poison ivy is everywhere; wild parsnip (yellow roadside flower) sap + sun causes blistering burns; never let kids chew unknown plants near water (water hemlock, the most poisonous plant in North America, grows in wet ditches here).
🦟 Bugs & water critters: mosquitoes and black flies peak in late June; leeches and "swimmer's itch" turn up in warm shallow lakes (rinse/towel off after swimming).
⛈️ Weather & signal: prairie and Great-Lakes storms blow up fast — check radar. Cell signal is spotty around Ely/Boundary Waters, the Shield near Sioux Narrows, and the Whiteshell; download offline maps + this page.

🛂 Border crossings & pets

Two crossings: into Canada Sat Jun 27 (International Falls → Fort Frances); back to the US Fri Jul 3 (Sprague → Warroad/Roseau). Have everyone's passports out and reachable.
🐕 Dog — into Canada: Canada requires a valid rabies vaccination certificate (in English/French, signed by your vet, identifying the dog) for dogs over 3 months. Bring the paper copy with the microchip number on it.
🐕 Dog — back into the US: since Aug 2024 the CDC requires a free CDC Dog Import Form for every dog (must be 6+ months and microchipped). Fill it out online a few days before Jul 3 — you'll get a receipt (valid 6 months) to show or print. Keep the rabies certificate handy too.
🚫 Don't carry across: no fresh produce, plants, or raw meat either direction — and no firewood (buy it at each park; moving it spreads tree-killing pests). Declare any alcohol/tobacco.
⛽ Fuel & cash: top off US-side before crossing (cheaper, and gas thins out in Ontario); a little Canadian cash helps at the cottage-area stores.

🗺️ Directions ~5 hr (≈290 mi), I-90/94

🦫 Wild Things — Chippewa River country

  • Beaver: second-biggest rodent on Earth; orange front teeth are iron-coated and never stop growing. Gross: marks territory with castoreum, a goo squeezed from sacs next to the butt that smells like vanilla — once used in perfume and food flavoring.
  • River otter: holds its breath ~8 min; the whole family shares one bathroom spot on the bank called a "latrine."
  • Bald eagle: builds the biggest tree nest of any N. American bird (up to 2 tons). Darker side: it's a scavenger and a thief — it dive-bombs ospreys to rob their fish and rips into rotting deer carcasses and roadkill.
  • Lake sturgeon: a living fossil, armored in bony plates, can live 100+ years and top 6 feet, vacuuming food off the river bottom.

⚠️ Heads-up — Chippewa Valley

  • Ticks & Lyme: first night in northwoods brush — repellent + a full tick check on everyone (and the dog) before bed.
  • Wild parsnip lines these roadsides (tall yellow flower); its sap + sunlight = blistering chemical burns. Look, don't touch.
  • Poison ivy along the river trails — "leaves of three, let it be."

🧭 Did you know? — Chippewa Valley, WI

  • Cornell has the world's only surviving "pulpwood stacker" — a 175-foot tower built in 1912 to pile logs for a paper mill.
  • Nearby Chippewa Falls is home to Leinenkugel's, the 7th-oldest brewery in the U.S. (1867), and the birthplace of Seymour Cray, the "father of the supercomputer."
  • Wisconsin is the "Badger State" — named not for the animal but for 1800s lead miners who dug into hillsides like badgers.
  • You'll follow the Chippewa River, one of the longest rivers entirely inside Wisconsin.

Fri, Jun 26 — Brunet Island → Bear Head Lake State Park, MN

🗺️ Directions ~4 hr (≈210 mi) through Duluth
Fuel/snacks: top off in Duluth (last big Kwik Trip cluster before the Northwoods)
Lunch + cool stop: Duluth Canal Park — eat with a Lake Superior view + the Aerial Lift Bridge; quick add-on is Enger Tower.
Dinner (takeout): Junction Bar & Grill, Babbitt (~10 min from camp), or pick up in Ely.
Cool stop near camp (Ely): International Wolf Center & North American Bear Center — both highly rated.

🐺 Wild Things — Northwoods & Boundary Waters

  • Gray wolf: smells prey 1.5+ miles off and gulps ~20 lbs of meat at once ("wolfing it down"). Gross: parents swallow meat, trot to the den, and vomit it back up for the pups; scat is full of hair and bone. Darker side: a pack kills by running prey to exhaustion and often starts eating the gut and organs before the animal is dead.
  • Moose: largest deer alive; eats 40–60 lbs of plants a day and dives fully underwater (nostrils closed) to feed off the lake bottom. The flap under its throat is a "bell."
  • Black bear: eats up to 20,000 calories/day before winter, then dens for months without eating, drinking, peeing, or pooping — a hard "fecal plug" seals its rear all winter. Nose ~7× a bloodhound's.
  • Common loon: solid (not hollow) bones let it dive 200 ft; legs set so far back it needs a quarter-mile water "runway" to take off; swallows pebbles to grind fish in its gut.

⚠️ Heads-up — bear & moose country, cold lake

  • Bear protocol at camp: all food, trash, and scented items locked in the car — never in the tent. A fed bear is a dangerous bear.
  • Moose > bears for injuries: give any moose a very wide berth, especially a cow with a calf; back away if ears pin back or hackles rise.
  • Lake Superior at Duluth/Canal Park: ~40°F, cold-water shock and rip currents — kids and dog stay out of the surf.
  • Dusk drive into Ely: peak moose/deer-on-road hours; slow down. Cell signal drops near camp.

🧭 Did you know? — Lake Superior & the Northwoods

  • Lake Superior is the largest freshwater lake on Earth by surface area — it holds about 10% of all the planet's surface fresh water and is so big it makes its own weather.
  • It's also the deepest Great Lake (1,333 ft / 406 m) and the third-largest lake in the world by volume — enough water to flood all of North and South America a foot deep.
  • Far out in the lake, Isle Royale hosts the longest-running predator–prey study on Earth (wolves and moose, since 1958); the moose swam ~15 miles to reach the island and the first wolves crossed on a winter ice bridge.
  • Duluth is the farthest-inland seaport in North America — ocean ships sail 2,300+ miles to reach it from the Atlantic.
  • You're crossing the Iron Range, where much of America's iron ore was mined for steel.
  • Ely is the gateway to the Boundary Waters, home to the largest wolf population in the lower 48 states — plus moose, black bears, and loons.

🗺️ Directions ~5 hr (≈230 mi) + border at International Falls / Fort Frances

Dinner (takeout): Snackhouse Restaurant, Sioux Narrows (burgers, fries) — 807-226-9753. Backup: The Cornerstone.

🐟 Wild Things — Lake of the Woods & the Canadian Shield

  • American white pelican: 9-ft wingspan, throat pouch holds 3 gallons; a line of them herds fish into the shallows to scoop. Gross: a chick eats by shoving its whole head down the parent's throat for regurgitated fish soup.
  • Muskie & walleye: the muskie ("fish of 10,000 casts") is a toothy ambush predator that eats ducklings and other fish whole. Walleye have mirror-backed eyes that glow and hunt in the dark.
  • Snapping turtle: too big to hide in its shell, so it bites — a beak that can take a finger. Darker side: it's the lake's cleanup crew, eating drowned and rotting animals off the bottom.
  • Jack pine: its cones are glued shut with resin and only pop open in a wildfire's heat — the tree needs fire to reproduce.

⚠️ Heads-up — border & remote Shield

  • Border: passports out; no fresh produce, plants, or meat across. Fuel up US-side (cheaper, and gas/cell thin out in Ontario).
  • Long remote stretches with little signal — top off the tank and download maps.
  • Biting black flies and mosquitoes are at their worst now; bug spray + long sleeves at dusk.
  • Watch for moose on the road through the Shield, especially low light.

🧭 Did you know? — The Border & Lake of the Woods

  • International Falls is nicknamed the "Icebox of the Nation" for some of the coldest winter temperatures in the continental U.S.
  • Crossing into Ontario, you roll onto the Canadian Shield — the largest area of exposed Precambrian rock on Earth, covering roughly half of Canada. Its bedrock runs up to a few billion years old; the Acasta Gneiss is ~4 billion.
  • The Shield's Sudbury Basin is a 1.85-billion-year-old meteorite crater — the second-largest impact structure on Earth, visible from space — whose debris was flung as far as Minnesota. Apollo astronauts trained there, and it's now one of the world's great nickel-mining districts.
  • Lake of the Woods has 14,522 islands — trace every island and the shoreline runs ~65,000 miles. Sioux Narrows sits right on it, and the lake's rocky shores hold ancient First Nations pictographs — some of the oldest rock art in Canada.
  • Watch for bald eagles, loons, and muskie in these waters.

🐍 Wild Things — where the forest becomes prairie

  • Red-sided garter snake: a couple hours away near Narcisse is the biggest snake gathering on the planet — limestone pits holding tens of thousands. Each spring males swarm each female into a writhing "mating ball." Gross: they give birth to live young (no eggs) and smear a foul musk on you when grabbed. By summer they've spread out to hunt frogs and worms.
  • Richardson's ground squirrel: the "gophers" of the fields — they sleep through both the cold AND the hottest weeks, up to 9 months a year underground.
  • Red-tailed hawk: hunts the fields with eyesight ~8× sharper than yours.

⚠️ Heads-up — arriving at Lake Winnipeg

  • 🐕 Blue-green algae — biggest dog risk of the trip. Lake Winnipeg blooms with toxic cyanobacteria in summer; a tiny amount can kill a dog within hours, no antidote. Before letting him near the water, check it's clear — no pea-soup green, scum, or paint-like streaks — and don't let him drink lakewater at the shoreline. Nearest 24-hr vet is in Winnipeg (~1.5 hr).
  • Swimmer's itch can show up in warm shallows; towel off after swimming.
  • Prairie thunderstorms build fast in the afternoon — keep an eye on the radar.

🧭 Did you know? — Victoria Beach & Lake Winnipeg

  • Victoria Beach is the only community in North America that bans private cars in July and August — you park outside and walk, bike, or take a cab to the cottage.
  • Its winter population of about 227 swells to roughly 10,000 people in summer.
  • It sits on Lake Winnipeg, one of the ten largest freshwater lakes in the world — shallow, so it warms up nicely for swimming.
  • You're crossing from rocky Canadian Shield into flat prairie — the bed of an Ice-Age lake bigger than all the Great Lakes combined.

Takeout / treats: Le Gouter, Village Green Bakery, Salty's Drive-Inn.

🐸 Wild Things — lake, marsh & boreal forest

  • Great blue heron: spears fish and swallows them whole, headfirst — sometimes you can watch a too-big lump slide down its neck. Gross: it blasts its poop backward away from the nest ("whitewash") and coughs up pellets of undigested bones and scales.
  • Wood frog: survives winter by letting two-thirds of its body freeze solid — heart stopped, not breathing — then thaws alive in spring using sugar as antifreeze.
  • Leeches: in the warm shallows. Gross: a leech has three jaws and ~100 tiny teeth and spit that stops blood from clotting so it can keep feeding (most lake leeches actually eat snails, not people — but check ankles after wading).
  • The rock: the pink-and-gray Whiteshell granite is billion-year-old Shield bedrock flecked with sparkly quartz and dark-red garnet.

⚠️ Heads-up — five days on the lake

  • 🐕 Keep checking the water for algae every beach day — blooms come and go with wind and heat. When in doubt, keep the dog out and rinse him if he's been in.
  • Ticks in the Whiteshell woods (Lyme) — nightly checks on hikers and the dog.
  • Black bears live in the Whiteshell — secure food and garbage at the cottage and on picnics.
  • Long, intense sun on open water — sunscreen and shade, especially midday.

🧭 Did you know? — Manitoba

  • Winnie-the-Pooh is named after Winnipeg! In 1914 a Canadian soldier, Harry Colebourn, bought a bear cub, named her "Winnie" after his hometown, and gave her to the London Zoo — where she inspired the stories.
  • The nearby Whiteshell has ancient Indigenous petroforms — rocks laid out into shapes of snakes, turtles, and people, some thousands of years old.
  • Winnipeg's The Forks has been a meeting place where two rivers join for 6,000+ years.
  • Manitoba is nicknamed the "Land of 100,000 Lakes" — even more lakes than Minnesota claims!

Fri, Jul 3 — Victoria Beach → Zippel Bay State Park, MN 🇺🇸

🗺️ Directions ~4 hr (≈250 km) via Sprague → Warroad/Roseau
Fuel/snacks: first Kwik Trip back in the US at Warroad or Roseau
Lunch: Warroad or Roseau
Dinner (takeout): Lake of the Woods Pizza Company, Baudette. Fast backup: McDonald's/Subway in Baudette.

🦅 Wild Things — Zippel Bay & Lake of the Woods

  • Piping plover: Zippel Bay's white-sand beach is one of only four places in the region this endangered shorebird still nests. When a predator nears the eggs, the parent fakes a "broken wing," flopping away to lure it off, then flies away fine.
  • Sandhill crane: nests on the bay's north side, stands 4+ ft tall, and paints itself by smearing rusty mud onto its feathers for camouflage. Darker side: it's an omnivore that stabs and eats ducklings, frogs, and rodents, and will raid other birds' nests for eggs. Its bugle carries 2.5 miles.
  • Also here: white pelicans, bald eagles, river otters, and — in the woods — timber wolves and moose.

⚠️ Heads-up — protected beach & border

  • Piping plover nesting: stay out of posted/fenced beach zones and keep the dog leashed away from them — disturbing nests is illegal and the birds are barely hanging on.
  • Border back into the US: passports, no produce/meat. Reset the dog's food plan after the crossing.
  • Ticks in the grass-backed beach and woods — nightly checks.

🧭 Did you know? — Warroad, Roseau & Baudette

  • Lake of the Woods — your campsite's lake — straddles Minnesota, Ontario, and Manitoba and is nicknamed "the other Great Lake." It averages just ~26 ft deep but plunges to 210 ft.
  • Warroad is "Hockeytown USA" — a tiny town that has sent players to multiple Olympics, and it's the home of Marvin Windows.
  • Roseau is the birthplace of the snowmobile and of Polaris — every Polaris sled in the world is still assembled there.
  • Baudette is the self-declared "Walleye Capital of the World," guarded by Willie the Walleye, a 40-foot, 2½-ton fish statue.
  • Just east is the Northwest Angle — the only piece of the lower-48 U.S. north of the Canada line, and you can only drive there through Canada.

🗺️ Directions ~4.5 hr (≈250 mi)

🦅 Wild Things — North Shore cliffs

  • Peregrine falcon: nests on the North Shore's lava cliffs and is the fastest animal alive — it dives ("stoops") over 200 mph, with bony cones in its nostrils so the wind doesn't blow its lungs apart. Darker side: it strikes prey in mid-air with a closed fist of talons and can knock the head clean off a pigeon, then plucks and eats it.
  • Lake Superior agate: Minnesota's state gem — quartz that filled gas bubbles in the billion-year-old lava, then tumbled smooth by glaciers. Hunt the gravel beach at Gooseberry.
  • Black bear: common all along the shore. Gross: its poop changes color with the season — dark from meat and bugs, then purple and seedy after gorging on berries.

⚠️ Heads-up — July 4 on the North Shore

  • Lake Superior again: cold-water shock + rip currents are deadly serious here — wade only, no swimming out, and watch the kids near the surf.
  • Waterfall edges: Gooseberry's wet rock ledges are slick with no railings — keep kids back from the lip and the gorge.
  • Holiday crowds = bolder, food-conditioned bears near busy campgrounds; lock up all food. Order dinner early.

🧭 Did you know? — Bemidji & the North Shore

  • Bemidji calls itself the "first city on the Mississippi River" and built the giant Paul Bunyan & Babe the Blue Ox statues back in 1937.
  • The North Shore's dramatic cliffs are ~1.1-billion-year-old lava flows from a time when North America almost ripped in half (the Midcontinent Rift).
  • Scan the beaches for Lake Superior agates — Minnesota's state gemstone, banded red-and-white and polished by glaciers.
  • Gooseberry Falls drops over those ancient lava ledges in a series of waterfalls you can walk right up to.

🗺️ Directions short day, ~1.5 hr (≈75 mi) — lots of slack for sightseeing
Cool stop (8 min from camp): Split Rock Lighthouse — iconic view, dog-friendly grounds + paved Gitchi-Gami Trail.
Fuel/snacks: big Kwik Trip selection in Duluth/Superior
Lunch: Duluth (Canal Park) or Superior
Dinner (takeout): Kro Bar & Grill, Brule; Culver's / A&W in Superior are dog-friendly fallbacks.

🐟 Wild Things — the Brule River

  • Brook trout: the spring-fed Brule is ice-cold, which is exactly what trout need — they only survive in clean, cold, oxygen-rich water, so a healthy trout stream proves the water is pure.
  • Sea lamprey: the river-and-lake horror story — an invasive jawless eel that clamps onto a fish with a suction mouth full of rasping teeth, files a hole, and drinks its blood and body fluids until the fish dies. It nearly wiped out Lake Superior's trout.
  • Crayfish: the mini-lobsters under the rocks — they can drop a claw to escape and regrow it, and swim backward by flipping their tail.

⚠️ Heads-up — cliffs & cold current

  • Split Rock cliff: 130-ft drop with open, unfenced edges in spots — keep a hand on the kids near the overlooks.
  • The Brule is a fast, cold river with real current — life jackets if anyone's near the water; it's not a wade-in swimming hole.
  • Lake Superior shoreline at Duluth/Superior: still ~40°F and current-prone.

🧭 Did you know? — Split Rock & the Brule

  • Split Rock Lighthouse was built because a single furious storm in November 1905 wrecked or damaged 29 ships on Lake Superior in one night.
  • Lake Superior's most famous wreck, the Edmund Fitzgerald, went down in a November storm in 1975 with all 29 hands ("...the lake, it is said, never gives up her dead"). An estimated 550 shipwrecks lie on the bottom, and the lake still holds the only naturally self-sustaining lake-trout population left in the Great Lakes.
  • The Brule River is the "River of Presidents" — five U.S. presidents (Grant, Cleveland, Coolidge, Hoover, Eisenhower) fished here; Coolidge ran his "Summer White House" from the Brule in 1928.
  • The spring-fed Brule drops 420 feet on its way to Lake Superior, making lots of rapids — it's a famous trout and salmon river.

🦤 Wild Things — Baraboo & the Dells gorge

  • Cranes: nearby Baraboo has the International Crane Foundation — the only place on Earth to see all 15 crane species. The whooping crane dropped to about 15 birds alive in 1941, and this place helped bring it back.
  • The gorge: a 5-mile canyon of 500-million-year-old sandstone, blasted open ~15,000 years ago when a glacier-dammed lake burst and tore through in days.
  • Little brown bat: eats up to 1,000 bugs an hour by echolocation. Darker side: a fungus called white-nose syndrome has killed millions of these bats — it grows on their faces while they hibernate and starves them awake.

⚠️ Heads-up — crowds, water & storms

  • The Dells is packed in July — typical tourist-crowd stuff (watch the kids in the throngs, mind water-park hygiene and water safety).
  • Severe afternoon thunderstorms are common in southern WI in summer — check radar before/after the water parks.
  • Mirror Lake construction (2026): a road-construction project at the park may affect some campsites, the boat launch, and parking lots — confirm site access at check-in and watch for detours.
  • Ticks in the Mirror Lake SP woods; quick check at camp.

🧭 Did you know? — Wisconsin Dells & Baraboo

  • The "Dells" is a 5-mile gorge of 500-million-year-old sandstone, carved when a glacial lake burst its ice dam ~15,000 years ago in a giant flood.
  • The land is sacred to the Ho-Chunk Nation, whose ancient burial mounds remain nearby.
  • Next-door Baraboo is the birthplace of the Ringling Brothers Circus (1884) — now the Circus World Museum with 260+ antique circus wagons.
  • Wisconsin Dells is also the self-proclaimed "Waterpark Capital of the World."

🌾 Wild Things — prairie & farm country home

  • Sandhill crane: flocks feed in southern Wisconsin's cut fields — one of the oldest bird types on Earth, barely changed in millions of years.
  • Turkey vulture: the V-winged bird teetering over the highway finds carcasses by smell from a mile up. Gross: it pees down its own legs to cool off and projectile-vomits rotten slurry at attackers — and its stomach acid is strong enough to destroy anthrax, cholera, and botulism in the rotten meat it eats.
  • Home stretch: the returning prairie is the flat bed of an ancient glacial lake — same story that started flattening out back in Manitoba.

⚠️ Heads-up — last leg

  • Summer storm season on the drive south — sudden downpours and crosswinds; build in buffer.
  • Deer near dawn/dusk on the rural stretches before home.

🧭 Did you know? — Madison & home

  • Wisconsin's capital, Madison, is built on a narrow isthmus — a skinny strip of land squeezed between two lakes, Mendota and Monona.
  • The Wisconsin State Capitol dome is the only granite dome in the U.S. and, by law, no building downtown may be taller than it.
  • Crossing back into Illinois, you're entering the "Prairie State" — almost home!
